## Done predicate

A committed, reproducible campaign must distinguish numerical checks, assumed input variation, boundary-model differences and measured physical accuracy. It must account for every requested native solve, preserve failures, test the actual coupon and the older published-paper interpretation separately, and report whether C220 versus C270 survives each declared domain. Every recommendation must identify the measurements needed to qualify its domain. A physical qualification claim without new measurements fails the predicate.

## Scope and throughput checkpoint

One baseline capture, two independent design sketches, one synthesized research harness, a bounded native scenario campaign, focused numerical controls, one independently reviewed report and a visible Lab handoff. Begin with roughly 100 to 250 native requests. Inspect the first paired cases before choosing the final campaign size. Keep source stable during computations. Do not deploy or alter frozen release, coupon or physical evidence. Do not rewrite the native optimizer.

## Acceptance checks before implementation

1. Nominal coupon requests reproduce the six archived planning results under their exact stated assumptions.
2. An independent physical-boundary experiment either passes unchanged numerical gates and volume/load controls or remains a retained negative result.
3. Native combined-input scenarios include both common and arm-specific deviations. No interval or probability guarantee follows from sampled points.
4. The report separates actual coupon geometry from the published four-pad comparison. No post-reflow observation enters a forward scenario.
5. Missing wetting, material, hot-to-cold and multi-joint information remains an explicit qualification gap.
6. The offline verifier checks requests, source and engine identities, all file hashes and regenerated conclusions. A changed conclusion or missing failed case is rejected.
7. Fresh native replay covers nominal and decision-critical cases. Existing application and coupon tests remain green.

## Completion and stopping decision

The final campaign used 61 distinct requests for 64 declared scenario-arm-boundary rows. Deduplication and decision-critical endpoint cases were sufficient to expose lost margins and reversed ordering. The original estimate of 100 to 250 requests was not a minimum acceptance gate. Six original planning cases and thirteen final replay cases were also repeated. Every requested native calculation has a retained result or explicit failure.

The finite-pad experiment tightens the geometry under prescribed wetting and tests relaxed-energy reactions. Its C270 moving-height failure and penetrating control remain negative results. A general active-contact model is not qualified. Twelve analytical rigid-cap cases identify a load-sharing and tilt sensitivity without claiming physical measurements.

Model the Domain separated liquid volume, artificial envelope volume, wetting hypotheses and shared versus arm-specific inputs. Build the Lever and Prove It Works produced a replayable campaign and an offline verifier. Explain the Number kept numerical tolerances separate from physical error bounds. The Laziness Protocol and Attack the Premise kept failed active-contact modeling in research and directed the next step toward wetting and volume observations rather than another optimizer layer.

The [final review](final-review.md) accepts the declared computational scope. The [measurement addendum](LAB_ADDENDUM.md) defines the remaining physical work. Neither pad choice has a qualified whole-cap clearance or force prediction. No public deployment or laboratory communication was performed.
