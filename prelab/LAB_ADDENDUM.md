# Measure the assumptions that change the coupon decision

This addendum supplements SS-C220-C270-R1. It does not change the frozen board files, allocation, historical predictions, fabrication status or acceptance rules. The requester has no lab equipment. An outside lab must quote and demonstrate the capabilities below before a confirmatory prediction freeze.

## Resolve the wetting boundary first

Record the finished height and radius of both copper pads, including the finish. Determine where the molten contact line sits at both ends. Distinguish top-edge pinning, partial sidewall wetting, and contact near the pad base. Record the observation method, temperature and uncertainty. Observe both pad-diameter arms. Do not assume a common wetting depth solely because both use the same finish process.

At the artificial nominal coupon inputs, the two established boundary interpretations differ by about 45 micrometers for C220 and 40 micrometers for C270. That changes the 185 micrometer numerical clearance outcome. The explicit finite-pad calculation is a conditional geometry experiment. It does not identify which boundary the assembly takes.

A cold section can characterize the cooled specimen. It cannot independently establish the molten contact line or hot-to-cold transformation. If hot wetting cannot be observed, report that missing capability and keep the liquid-model claim unqualified. Do not fit a contact depth to the final measured gap and score that same gap as independent validation.

## Identify liquid volume independently of height

Weigh each clean cap and each assigned twenty-sphere kit. Record individual sphere dimensions and placement when possible. Use a sourced or measured liquid density at the recorded plateau temperature to convert the assigned solder mass into liquid volume. Document metal loss, dissolution and flux contributions where consequential. Sphere diameter alone gives a solid geometric volume. It is not a measurement of molten volume.

Keep the two arms' volume information separate. The native opposite-endpoint test reduces the predicted C220 advantage to 2.82 micrometers under rim pinning and 0.84 micrometers under the full-cylinder interpretation. Both are smaller than the requested 3 micrometer paired uncertainty. The wider opposite-volume stress reverses the prediction, but that plus or minus 5 percent volume stress is not a measured tolerance or the screened sphere band.

## Observe the whole cap

Return registered positions, local gaps, pad geometry and wetting evidence for all twenty joints. Preserve minima and maxima. A mean gap cannot establish the minimum finished-pad-face gap. Record cap bow, tilt, center of mass and any fixture or thermocouple contact that changes load. The cap assembly is the independent specimen. Twenty joints are not twenty independent replications.

Keep the existing guarded three micrometer gap-range gate. A deterministic reduced-order sphere-gradient calculation tests why it matters. A spatial gradient can rotate a free rigid cap even when all sphere diameters lie inside the requested geometric endpoints. It is a prescribed stress test, not a predicted manufacturing frequency.

## Separate the physical questions

1. For cooled clearance, measure the whole-cap minimum minus its justified expanded uncertainty against 185 micrometers. This can establish a result for the tested coupon process even if the liquid model is wrong.
2. For molten geometry, freeze predictions from incoming dimensions, measured masses, independent material information and identified wetting conditions before acquiring confirmatory gaps. Score held-out hot geometry against the existing five micrometer prediction-error target. That target is not a demonstrated error bound.
3. For force accuracy, qualify the separate hot-force fixture. Measure actual displacement, load and contact condition at more than one point. Agreement of a cooled height does not qualify restoring force.

A loaded equilibrium height alone cannot identify cap load and surface tension separately. In the no-gravity fixed-wetting model, multiplying both by the same factor leaves equilibrium shape unchanged. Weighing the cap and measuring material properties independently remove that confounding. A zero-load geometry observation plus a small load-displacement change is more informative than fitting several unknowns to one height.

## Staged quotation

Quote incoming metrology and a small pilot first. Confirm whether hot boundary observations and whole-cap metrology are feasible. Then freeze the process, prediction inputs and their uncertainty before the planned confirmation specimens. Preserve all failed or ineligible specimens and keep calibration specimens separate from confirmation. Do not order the force fixture solely to resolve a height-boundary ambiguity that the pilot can test more directly.

Return raw observations, calibration records, assigned sphere maps, hot traces, registered sections and a list of capabilities that could not be demonstrated. Missing inputs remain missing. No new physical observations exist in the pre-lab computational campaign.
