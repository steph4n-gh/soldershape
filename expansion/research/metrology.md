# Matching force metrology feasibility

Research checked 2026-10-16. This is an engineering feasibility assessment for an outside-lab RFQ. It is not vendor acceptance, a validated uncertainty budget, or authorization to fabricate.

## Decision

The force scale is measurable with commercial transducers. The complete molten-solder experiment still needs a lab integration and commissioning gate. Select one cool, calibrated, single-axis microforce sensor with a thermally isolated pad carrier. Measure lateral force first. Index the sensor and its adapter to measure normal force in separate specimens and blocks. Keep the solder joint horizontal with gravity along solver z in both configurations. Do not rotate the joint to repurpose the sensitive axis.

Prescribe the actual two rim positions through local optical metrology and closed-loop correction. A stage encoder measures its own carriage. It does not establish the rim separation after sensor deflection, carrier bending and thermal expansion. Keep the unmeasured degrees of freedom stiff. Characterize their compliance and the full axis response matrix with the assembled carrier. Do not add a second contact that bypasses the load cell.

Simultaneous hot two-axis sensing is a possible later integration. Public sources do not establish a combined low-range, two-axis, heated-tip specification suitable for this experiment. Do not label it off the shelf or build-ready.

## What must be discriminated

The repository's `demo/protocol.json` declares 200 µm circular pads, a 150 µm held gap, 5 and 7 nL, surface tension 0.48 N/m, density 9 g/cm3 and gravity enabled. Those material properties do not identify an actual alloy at a stated temperature. Preserve the old reference values as numerical reference predictions. Choose the actual alloy, independent material measurements and temperature before freezing physical-process predictions. If the measured inputs differ, recompute prospectively. Do not fit them to the measured joint forces.

The opening 120 µm-offset predictions are 92.263 and 82.193 µN lateral restoring magnitude. Their difference is 10.070 µN using rounded displayed values. The smaller normal prediction is +7.619 µN upward. For a useful approximately three-to-one discrimination margin, propose the following contractual gates. They are design choices rather than a universal validation standard.

| Quantity | Proposed commissioning gate | Consequence |
|---|---:|---|
| Corrected individual force measurement | Expanded uncertainty U at most 2 µN | A genuine +7.619 µN observation can exclude zero with useful margin. |
| Observed 5 versus 7 nL lateral contrast | U at most 3 µN | Two independent U=2 µN measurements give U=2.828 µN by quadrature. Actual covariance must be retained. |
| Contrast comparison with frozen prediction | Combined measurement and input-prediction U at most 10.070/3 = 3.357 µN | If measurement U is 2.828 µN, independent predicted-contrast U can be at most 1.808 µN. This may fail on material or geometry uncertainty. |
| Absolute normal comparison | Combined measurement and input-prediction U at most 3 µN | With measurement U=2 µN, independent prediction U can be at most 2.236 µN. Require the predicted sign to survive the measured-input envelope. |

Here U uses coverage factor k=2 only where the distribution and degrees of freedom justify it. Small specimen counts may require a larger factor. Propagate covariance between the two volumes, calibration, surface tension and geometry. Do not reduce shared bias by dividing by the square root of the sample count. NIST distinguishes standard and expanded uncertainty and requires the coverage choice to be documented. [NIST TN 1297 section 6](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-6-expanded-uncertainty), [NIST uncertainty approach](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section5/mpc52.htm).

Force-meter uncertainty and uncertainty of model inputs are separate. A force difference from uncertain rim geometry is not sensor error. Freeze both budgets. Sensitivity calculations at actual measured inputs must set the final allowable gap, diameter, volume, offset and surface-tension uncertainties. Submicrometer rim-position targets alone do not prove adequate force discrimination.

## Why the remote carrier needs verification

A numerical example bounds the mechanical choice. For a uniform fused-silica rod 20 mm long and 1 mm in diameter, the room-temperature cantilever estimate k=3EI/L³ gives 1,335 N/m. A 124 µN transverse force bends it about 0.093 µm. A 0.5 mm rod is sixteen times more compliant and bends about 1.49 µm. These estimates exclude sensor, joint, clamp, elbow and adhesive compliance. They are not an assembled-fixture certificate.

The 1 mm rod alone weighs about 34.6 mg, or 339 µN. It exceeds a ±200 µN cell in a vertical dead-load configuration before any solder force. Taring does not restore mechanical range. The selected sensor range must cover the full carrier mass, preload, thermal motion and force in both signs. A 20 mm length grows about 1.88 µm over a uniform 200 K change even with low-expansion fused silica. Never use room-temperature stage coordinates as the final hot gap. The calculations use manufacturer E=72.5 GPa, density 2.201 g/cm3 and mean expansion coefficient 0.47×10^-6/K. [Heraeus fused-silica properties](https://www.heraeus-covantics.com/brands/clear-fused-quartz).

At 124 µN, an uncorrected 0.5% axis leakage is 0.620 µN. A 0.1° axis misalignment leaks about 0.216 µN. A 1 µN gravity change is equivalent to about 0.102 mg. At the demo density, 7 nL of solder weighs about 0.618 µN. These are ordinary calculated force scales. Carrier mass, local gravitational acceleration, axis registration and how liquid weight enters the upper-pad reaction must be defined. Calibrate the sign with known positive and negative forces at the assembled pad, not by the sign of a stage command.

For a nominal one-sigma force-measurement allocation, calibration 0.35, hot zero drift 0.35, repeatability 0.25, residual cross-axis error 0.30, tare/parasitic correction 0.25, compliance correction 0.30 and other quantified terms 0.20 µN give U=1.536 µN at k=2 if independent. This is a planning allocation, not measured performance. It leaves some margin to U=2 µN. Unknown terms cannot be entered as zero. The lab must replace every term with certificates or controlled observations and include covariance where present.

## Commissioning and specimen boundaries

1. Confirm the hot custom attachment and moving load path with the lab and sensor supplier before purchasing or machining the final adapter. Keep sensor body, cables and precision stages in their rated environment. Characterize actual sensor temperature. Put a thermocouple near each pad on a non-force-bearing fixture. A thermocouple wire attached to the moving pad can be an unintended spring.
2. At room temperature and operating temperature, run no-bridge zero controls through the entire positioning sequence. Repeat with heat off/on and purge off/on. Quantify warm-up, steady drift, convection, cable forces and electrostatic effects. Temperature stability is a measured joint condition, not just a controller display.
3. Calibrate both signs across the actual operating range through the completed carrier. Apply representative cross-axis loads and torques. A deadweight, calibrated transfer sensor or electrostatic force standard needs traceable uncertainty appropriate to the selected geometry. Verify that the load path remains the same after indexing the sensing axis.
4. As initial engineering targets, require expanded uncertainty at most 0.5 µm in the hot relative rim gap and offset, at most 0.1° pad tilt, and at most 0.5 µm drift over a dwell. Then tighten them if the frozen sensitivity calculations exceed the force budget. A 0.1° tilt across a 200 µm pad produces about 0.349 µm gap variation. Tilt is not modeled, so it must be bounded, not silently absorbed by a force correction.
5. Build the boundary as a thin, measured, circular wettable island with a measured nonwetting surround. Avoid using a tall, bare copper post whose sidewall changes the contact line. Image both complete contact lines where possible. If either line leaves the specified rim, or cannot be observed well enough to establish it, classify the fixed-rim comparison as out of domain. Pad-metal volume and underside clearance require explicit geometry accounting.
6. Independently measure actual metal volume, alloy composition, liquid density and surface tension at the selected temperature and atmosphere. A preform's cold volume is not its liquid volume. Verify transferred material and residue. Flux adds an interface and can add forces. A flux-free process requires demonstrated oxide removal and stable wetting. Nitrogen alone does not establish oxide-free surfaces. Do not silently replace the modeled solder/gas interface with solder/flux interfacial tension.
7. Commission large signals first. Test lateral restoring forces and the larger normal-force contrast at 40 µm offset before attempting the small upward 7 nL/120 µm sign. Use independent specimens for separate-axis confirmation. An x result does not validate z. An index mechanism must preserve the joint's gravity orientation.
8. Dwell until both force and actual rim coordinates stabilize. Compare approach from opposite directions and a slower motion sequence. Preserve hysteresis and drift as observations. Failure to reach stable, repeatable behavior falsifies the proposed equilibrium measurement method. It is not grounds to average until agreement appears.

The Linkam manufacturer specifically permits argon and nitrogen purge but says not to use helium or hydrogen in its stages. A fluxless hydrogen/formic-acid process cannot be inserted into that chamber by assumption. The selected atmosphere and chamber chemistry need explicit supplier/lab acceptance. [Linkam stage support](https://www.linkam.co.uk/support/stages/general).

Published NIST work measured solder force-displacement relationships and reports sensitivity to contact-line departure and substantial small-specimen volume scatter. Its fitted surface tension/contact-angle exercise is not a precedent for fitting the current held-out validation outcomes. The experiment should measure those inputs independently. [Josell et al., original paper](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=853827).

## Stop conditions and cheapest decisive fallback

The first purchase decision should follow a quoted hot-blank and transfer-calibration demonstration. Stop the primary validation run if the assembled system misses U=2 µN, if a force-bearing temperature lead is needed without a quantified correction, if the actual gap cannot be held, if contact-line pinning fails, or if model-input uncertainty makes the force contrast ambiguous. Report inconclusive and retain the failed commissioning data.

If the 120 µm upward sign cannot be resolved, keep it as a deferred endpoint. A lateral-only measurement with a cool single-axis sensor and the same horizontal hot-joint geometry is the smaller decisive first experiment. Start at 40 µm with the larger normal contrast as a separate commissioning endpoint when available. It can test substantial restoring forces and boundary behavior. It cannot establish the +7.619 µN claim, simultaneous vector accuracy, assembly capture range or production yield.

Explain the Number separated advertised noise from whole-system uncertainty. It changed the recommendation from assumed hot two-axis capability to staged single-axis commissioning with measured uncertainty gates. No physical observations were acquired in this research slice.
