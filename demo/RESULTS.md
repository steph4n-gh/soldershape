# Why this demo is more useful than a nominal height table

The demo exposes an engineering tradeoff that a process engineer can inspect before deciding what to test. More solder increases volume by 40 percent, yet it reduces the lateral restoring force at the opening sample and reverses the vertical force direction. The objective is explicit and illustrative. Seek lateral restoration without an upward separating force while an external fixture holds the gap.

The real 36-pad KiCad footprint has 400 micrometer pitch and 200 micrometer circular pads. Only the selected joint is modeled. The gap is held at 150 micrometers. Liquid volumes are 0.005 and 0.007 cubic millimeters. Tension is 0.48 N/m, density is 9 g/cm3, and gravity is enabled. The footprint does not establish those material, solder-volume or fixture assumptions.

The seventh case compresses the 7 nL, 40 micrometer-offset joint from a 150 to a 60 micrometer held gap. Its native run reports warning 1825, Hessian not positive definite. No successful mesh or force is substituted. This is a numerical failure, not a prediction that a physical joint breaks.

## Why someone might care

A nominal aligned-height formula does not answer this nonzero-offset, fixed-gap lateral and axial force question. That makes the demo a stronger illustration of the existing engine's capability. The force-displacement problem is established prior work, including the [NIST study](https://www.nist.gov/publications/misaligned-flip-chip-solder-joints-prediction-and-experimental-determination-lateral). SolderShape adds an inspectable footprint context, explicit objective, negative cases and reproducible handoff. It has not discovered new physics or demonstrated customer demand.

The potential user is an engineer with a real fixture, joint geometry and process question outside ordinary package guidance. The useful action is to compare assumptions and design a measurement. Whether it saves that engineer an assembly iteration remains untested. The [product investment gate](../refinement/POSITIONING.md) still applies.

Physical accuracy remains unqualified. The earlier force-validation failure remains unresolved. These independent static equilibria do not predict alignment trajectories, initial wetting, capture range, solder bridges, thermal reflow or manufacturing yield. Neighboring joints and package load sharing are absent. A displayed full-diameter offset does not prove that solder can form that connected joint in a real assembly. The physical 220/270 micrometer lab coupon is a separate experiment and does not qualify these stress-case forces.

## Design and review

Two whole designs were compared before implementation. The focused recorded page won over an additional workbench mode. The independent judge scored them 19 versus 17 of20. Root scored them 19 versus16. Both selected the same base. Existing solver, bundle format, editable-case format and renderer were reused. No new dependency or second editor was added.

Independent reviews reproduced three defects in the new recorder and verifier. The cached builder could label old receipts with new Python source identities. The verifier could accept an unrelated native error as reproduction of the retained failure. The ZIP advertised a generic replay command that necessarily rejected its failed case. All were repaired. [repair-verification.md](repair-verification.md) gives the fresh independent controls. Both reviewers inherited the parent model, so their agreement is not evidence across model families.

The final application suite passed 60 tests in [product-tests.log](product-tests.log). The eight focused demo checks pass in [tests.log](tests.log). Recorded-byte integrity, normalized request binding and the three negative results are covered. No further software blocker was found in the inspected scope. Physical force accuracy and practitioner value remain open.

Model the Domain kept both results and their screen in one selected scenario. Experience First made the force consequence visible before editing controls. Build the Lever produced the recorder and verifier. Prove It Works required real browser exports and native replay. Explain the Number kept source identity, numerical agreement, model scope and physical uncertainty separate.
